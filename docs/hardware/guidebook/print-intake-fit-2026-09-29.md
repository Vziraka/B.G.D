## 0. Count, cool and clean

Count and name the seven pieces before fitting anything. Confirm with the printer/operator that they are **PETG**, fully cool, and have had brims and temporary supports removed. Look for cracks, split layers, warped flat faces, and broken thin walls. In particular, the Pi carrier needs underside support removed while its four tie tunnels stay open; the camera needs tree support removed from its base slots without tearing small screw bosses; the depth bracket needs its boss support removed. Check that the camera's **four** small bores and the depth bracket's **two** bores are open. The battery stops, stack adapter and flow cradle were prepared without supports in the combined project. Clear removable support using the printer operator's normal method, with the cutting direction away from hands; **do not force screws through a blocked bore or drill an electronics board**.

**Pass:** seven named, cool, intact PETG pieces with usable openings. **Stop:** a missing piece, unknown material, a crack, a broken boss/tunnel/foot, or support that cannot be safely cleared. Report the part name and defect in text; a targeted photo is useful only if the defect cannot be described.

## 1. Empty Pi carrier first — runbook A4

Take only the cleaned Pi carrier, four loose RDQ **150 × 3 mm** ties, the four Pololu **M2.5** standoffs/nuts, and the unpowered frame. Do not move the live desk Pi yet.

1. Slide one **loose** tie through each of the **four** printed tunnels; none should scrape or need force. Check the two underside bearing rails sit flat and are free of support.
2. Trial-seat the four M2.5 standoffs in the carrier slots with their nuts below. They must fit without enlarging printed slots. Nuts and protruding threads must remain **above** the plane of the bearing rails so they cannot scrape the frame.
3. Rest the two rails on **solid top-plate material**, not across a large hole or slot. The empty carrier must not rock. Trace each tie's path through a tunnel, down one frame slot, under a **solid** plate strip and up a second slot, with buckle heads below and clear of the future stack/wires. Leave the ties loose; check access for Pi cooler, camera ribbon and GPIO cable.

**Pass:** four clear tunnels, hardware seats, both rails bear on solid plate, and each tie has a non-sliding route without electronic contact. **Stop:** rocking, rail overhang, nut/bolt contact with frame, a blocked tunnel or no valid tie route. Report **which** of those occurred before installing the Pi or tightening a tie. The [carrier notes](../f450/pi-carrier-core-draft.md) give the current raised-rail geometry.

## 2. Stack adapter — fit before any motor-wire cut

**September 30 actual-height trial now available:** the owner reports the empty adapter fits flat with four usable holes. The earlier board photos show black silicone corner grommets already seated in all four corners of both the original MicoAir743 FC and 50 A ESC; the owner previously reported four M3 × 25 mm bolts and four locknuts in hand. With USB/battery/charger disconnected and props off, work on a clean nonmetal surface: lift the empty adapter off the frame, place four M3 × 25 mm bolts **upward from its recessed underside**, then lower the ESC (board with twelve motor pads and wide battery tabs) onto the bolts through its four existing rubber grommets. Keep battery pads, motor pads and eight-pin connector accessible. Lower the MicoAir743 FC above it through its existing rubber grommets, with the FC's printed forward arrow aimed between the frame's two red front arms. Rotate the **ESC only** as needed so its short eight-pin harness can later reach both sockets without crossing a motor pad; do not force or plug the harness in this dry trial. Check that boards touch only the rubber mounting sleeves, never each other's components or bare metal; the boards must not bend. Thread the four M3 locknuts loosely by hand to test engagement; stop if the 25 mm bolts cannot reach the nylon inserts without rubber crush or if any metal touches a PCB component. Return the loose assembly to the supported adapter position and trial-place the upper frame plate. Require at least 5 mm between the plate and the highest nut/thread/board/cable, plus available motor-pad access and adapter tie passages. Do not cut motor leads, tighten ties or connect power yet. Report board separation, nut engagement and measured or clearly visible upper-plate clearance before final fastening.

Put the **empty** stack adapter at frame center on the lower plate. Its broad feet need solid plate support; none may bridge an opening. Keep future metal fasteners away from exposed power-distribution copper. Check four tie passages can reach real frame slots/webs without crossing ESC pads or future cables. The [electronics notes](../f450/electronics-design.md) select **four M3 × 25 mm** prototype bolts with the stack's silicone dampers and nylock nuts, but those 25 mm bolts were **not confirmed purchased**. Do not substitute the owned 30 mm bolts by assumption or clamp bare boards to plastic.

Next, count whether four M3 × 25 mm bolts, four nylock nuts and eight intact silicone dampers are physically available. The owner reported the bolts/nuts in hand, and the earlier board photos show the eight dampers installed; confirm they remain intact before the guided **unpowered actual-height trial above**. If the stack was shipped preassembled, leave it intact rather than separating boards to repeat that trial. The trial checks that the ESC sits **below** the original FC, bolt heads are isolated from lower-plate copper, motor pads and the short FC–ESC connector remain accessible, nothing is crushed, and the complete assembly including nut/thread tips leaves **at least 5 mm** below the refitted upper plate. Recheck the one-arm motor route from the [received-stack card](stack-and-motor-route-dry-fit-2026-09-28.md) at that **actual** supported ESC height and orientation before cutting. Do not cut, strip or solder the motor leads in this intake check.

**Pass for this intake:** stable supported **empty** adapter, four viable tie routes, and a count of the actual bolts/nuts/dampers. **Later stack-fit gate:** protected bolt heads, accessible pads/cable, ≥5 mm measured upper-plate clearance and a recorded one-arm route. **Stop:** unavailable correct bolts, adapter slips/rocks, any metal-to-copper contact, pinched wiring, damaged dampers, board bend or <5 mm clearance. Report the specific conflict; the calculated 25 mm fit is not physical proof.

## 3. Battery stops and straps

Check both end stops have smooth battery-contact edges and intact slotted feet. With the battery **unplugged**, place the existing grip pad and the two stops around the already dry-fitted pack; do not trap its main or balance lead. The stops limit fore/aft movement; **two transverse straps** carry/hold the pack. Try the owned 16 × 250 mm straps through real plate slots. Require at least **30 mm of engaged hook-and-loop overlap** on each after normal buckle routing, with secured tails, no pack deformation and no part of a strap crossing the downward sensor's view. The design selected 16 × 300 mm straps if the existing pair cannot achieve that overlap; do not stretch short straps to declare a pass. Each stop ultimately needs opposed tie paths resisting slide in both directions, with solid foot support. Keep ≥30 mm static ground clearance for the loaded battery/gear, with flex still to be tested.

**Pass:** smooth stops supported on plate, cables free, two normal straps meet the 30 mm overlap target, and opposed stop-tie paths exist. **Stop:** short strap, sliding stop, sharp contact, pinched lead, battery distortion or low ground gap. Use a rigid weighted dummy, **not the LiPo**, for any firm retention/pull trial. See [battery notes](../f450/battery-design.md).

## 4. Camera and forward-depth brackets

Use the **six small printed bores** found in Check 0. Set the unpowered Camera Module 3 and Pololu 3418 depth board against their separate bosses without forcing either PCB. The candidate hardware is **six M2 × 10 mm screws plus M2 nuts**, which was not in the confirmed order. The purchased **M2.5** screws are too large: never thread them into these boards. If M2 hardware is absent, mark mounting pending; the loose visual fit can still check lens/depth openings, compact screw-head space, camera ribbon path and depth header access. Bases need broad contact on the lower front plate and two independent tie routes each through solid frame webs, clear of propeller sweep, power pads and optics. Do not attach a board with glue or a tie across components.

**Pass:** all bores/bosses intact, both boards bear only at mounting regions, optical faces and cable exits are open, base/tie paths are supported; final fastener pass requires actual M2 engagement. **Stop:** boss crack, blocked hole, pressure on lens/chip/ribbon latch, oversized screw, or unsupported base. See [camera/depth notes](../f450/camera-design.md).

## 5. Downward MTF-01P cradle

Place the **intact cased** sensor in its shallow cradle; do not remove its case screws as mounting screws. Pass the proposed two 150 × 3 mm ties **loosely** around the case at the cradle guides. Confirm neither tie can slide over an optical barrel/aperture or the connector, and that battery, stops, straps and landing legs leave the downward view open. Do not crush the case to compensate for a loose print.

**Pass:** sensor sits within its locating walls, both potential tie paths retain the case without covering optics/connector, and the downward view is clear. **Stop:** optical obstruction, case pressure, unsupported cradle or no tie path. Actual optical quality still needs a later powered sensor test, not this fit check.
