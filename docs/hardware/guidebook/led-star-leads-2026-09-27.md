# LED star leads — isolated solder task, revision 1 (September 27, 2026)

**Scope:** prepare the two received Cree XM-L2 white 20 mm LED star boards for the required forward/downward lights. This releases only four low-voltage lead-to-star solder joints, one red and one black per board. It does **not** release the BuckPuck driver, connection to the aircraft, heatsink/optic retention, or powered testing. Build and check the first star before repeating on the second. The aircraft battery, charger and Pi stay disconnected; propellers stay off.

## Why this can be done before Monday

The [live BOM](../../increment-1-2-bom.md) records two `CREEXML2-W318` stars and three red/black 20 AWG foot-pairs received by owner report. [LEDSupply's exact star page](https://www.ledsupply.com/leds/cree-xlamp-xm-l2-leds) describes an electrically isolated 20 mm metal-core board with four solder pads and identifies the 10734 holder/Carclo optic. The finished harness needs one positive and one negative connection on each star regardless of the later series wiring. Allocate **one 100 mm red and one 100 mm black 20 AWG lead per star** (400 mm total wire, 200 mm of each color). This leaves about 715 mm of each color from the purchased three foot-pairs for the remainder of the planned low-current harness. These short pigtails permit later insulated splices; their length does not set the final front-to-down lamp spacing.

The [SunnySky motor-route review](../../research/build-compatibility-recheck-2026-09-20.md) shows that motor extensions have only 0–15 mm nominal margin and depend on the incoming ESC position. The EC5/current-sensor and Pi-power harnesses have unresolved exact routing/pin/strain-relief details. None of those is a suitable first permanent joint today. This light-board preparation is useful for the full product but does not move the first daylight-hover gate.

## Before touching the iron

Have one received **white XM-L2 20 mm star**, the received **20 AWG red/black** wire, cutters/stripper, YIHUA station with a small chisel tip, included lead-free solder, electronics flux, helping hands, eye protection, ventilated heat-resistant work area, and multimeter. Use the stand. Keep food and drink away. Do not use the battery or BuckPuck to test the bare board.

Check the star's `+` and `−` pad markings against its front side, and briefly rest the **10734 holder** over it without fastening anything. Choose one `+` pad and one `−` pad left accessible by the holder and with wire exit away from the optical center. LEDSupply says there are four pads; the exact pad pair and holder orientation are established by the **received board's printed polarity marks**, not by wire color or a similar-looking image. If the board is not a single white LED star or the pad markings are absent/unclear, stop before soldering; report the words/symbols you see. A targeted photo is useful only if the markings cannot be described.

## One-board procedure

1. With the iron off, cut **one 100 mm red** and **one 100 mm black** section from the purchased 20 AWG pair. Strip about **3 mm at the star end only** of each. Leave the far ends insulated. Keep the unused wire and second star away from the work area.
2. Clamp the **metal-board edge** gently in the helping hands without touching the LED dome or pads. Remove the holder. Fit the chisel tip while the iron is cold. Power only the soldering station; start at **350 °C** with the included lead-free solder, a practical starting setting rather than a verified alloy-specific requirement. Use the setting that produced sound practice joints if it is nearby. Keep the tip clean and lightly tinned.
3. Put a small amount of electronics flux on the selected `+` and `−` pads. Briefly tin each pad and the stripped end of each wire separately. If solder does not flow onto a pad after a few seconds of good contact, lift the iron and let the board cool; review tip contact/cleanliness rather than holding heat on the LED.
4. Hold the red wire flat on the tinned **`+`** pad. Touch the iron to wire and pad just long enough for both solder surfaces to flow together. Remove heat and keep the wire still until solid. Repeat with black on **`−`**. Keep the two wires and solder off the LED dome, holder-foot holes, board edge and other pad. [Adafruit's soldering examples](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-guide-excellent-soldering/common-problems) show the difference between wetted, disturbed and bridged joints.
5. Turn the station off; let the star cool in the holder. Check that solder has wetted each pad and wire, with no loose strands or bridge. Hold the board and give each insulated lead a **gentle** tug: no movement at its solder pad. Place the 10734 holder loosely again; it must not pinch the wires or touch the LED dome.
6. With the board disconnected from everything, meter-check each free wire end to **its own pad**: near the same resistance as touching the probes together. Check there is **no hard zero-ohm short between `+` and `−` in both probe orientations**; an LED can make simple beeper/diode readings direction-dependent, so a beep alone is not a universal failure. If the result is unclear, leave the board unpowered and report the meter's displayed readings. Cover/store the free wire ends separately so they cannot contact one another or loose metal.

**Pass for this one board:** both polarities match printed markings; both joints are wetted and immobile; holder clears wires; each wire reaches its pad; no hard two-way short. **Fail/stop:** unclear pad polarity, damaged dome/board, lifted pad, loose wire, solder bridge, or ambiguous meter readings. Do not connect the 4S pack or the driver to diagnose it. Once the first board passes, repeat with the second star and another red/black 100 mm pair. Label the two completed boards `front` and `down` for later layout. Their heatsinks, optics, series link and powered driver test remain unreleased until the mounting/wiring package is checked.
