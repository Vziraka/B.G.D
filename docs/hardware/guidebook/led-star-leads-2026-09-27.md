# LED star leads and heatsinks — isolated module task, revision 3 (September 29, 2026)

**Scope:** prepare the two received Cree XM-L2 white 20 mm LED star boards for the required forward/downward lights. This releases four low-voltage lead-to-star solder joints, one red and one black per board. A meter may be unavailable during isolated soldering: in that case record each star as **soldered, awaiting meter check**, with its free ends separately insulated. Bonding to each matching **HS13137 heatsink's supplied adhesive tape** follows only after its electrical check passes and the tape is intact. This does **not** release the BuckPuck driver, connection to the aircraft, optic-holder attachment, heatsink-to-aircraft retention, or powered testing. Build and visually/mechanically check the first star before repeating on the second. The aircraft battery, charger and Pi stay disconnected; propellers stay off.

## Why this can be done before the prints arrive

The [live BOM](../../increment-1-2-bom.md) records two `CREEXML2-W318` stars and three red/black 20 AWG foot-pairs received by owner report. [LEDSupply's exact star page](https://www.ledsupply.com/leds/cree-xlamp-xm-l2-leds) describes an electrically isolated 20 mm metal-core board with four solder pads and identifies the 10734 holder/Carclo optic. The finished harness needs one positive and one negative connection on each star regardless of the later series wiring. Allocate **one 100 mm red and one 100 mm black 20 AWG lead per star** (400 mm total wire, 200 mm of each color). This leaves about 715 mm of each color from the purchased three foot-pairs for the remainder of the planned low-current harness. These short pigtails permit later insulated splices; their length does not set the final front-to-down lamp spacing.

The [SunnySky motor-route review](../../research/build-compatibility-recheck-2026-09-20.md) shows that motor extensions have only 0–15 mm nominal margin and depend on the incoming ESC position. The EC5/current-sensor and Pi-power harnesses have unresolved exact routing/pin/strain-relief details. None of those is a suitable first permanent joint today. This light-board preparation is useful for the full product but does not move the first daylight-hover gate.

## Before touching the iron

Have one received **white XM-L2 20 mm star**, the received **20 AWG red/black** wire, cutters/stripper, YIHUA station with a small chisel tip, included lead-free solder, electronics flux, helping hands, eye protection, and a ventilated heat-resistant work area. Use the stand. Keep food and drink away. Do not use the battery or BuckPuck to test the bare board. The multimeter is required before electrical qualification, heatsink bonding or any power, but the disconnected lead joints can be made while it is away from the bench.

Check the star's `+` and `−` pad markings against its front side, and briefly rest the **10734 holder** over it without fastening anything. Choose one `+` pad and one `−` pad left accessible by the holder and with wire exit away from the optical center. LEDSupply says there are four pads; the exact pad pair and holder orientation are established by the **received board's printed polarity marks**, not by wire color or a similar-looking image. If the board is not a single white LED star or the pad markings are absent/unclear, stop before soldering; report the words/symbols you see. A targeted photo is useful only if the markings cannot be described.

## One-board procedure

1. With the iron off, cut **one 100 mm red** and **one 100 mm black** section from the purchased 20 AWG pair. Strip about **3 mm at the star end only** of each. Leave the far ends insulated. Keep the unused wire and second star away from the work area.
2. Clamp the **metal-board edge** gently in the helping hands without touching the LED dome or pads. Remove the holder. Fit the chisel tip while the iron is cold. Power only the soldering station; start at **350 °C** with the included lead-free solder, a practical starting setting rather than a verified alloy-specific requirement. Use the setting that produced sound practice joints if it is nearby. Keep the tip clean and lightly tinned.
3. Put a small amount of electronics flux on the selected `+` and `−` pads. Briefly tin each pad and the stripped end of each wire separately. If solder does not flow onto a pad after a few seconds of good contact, lift the iron and let the board cool; review tip contact/cleanliness rather than holding heat on the LED.
4. Hold the red wire flat on the tinned **`+`** pad. Touch the iron to wire and pad just long enough for both solder surfaces to flow together. Remove heat and keep the wire still until solid. Repeat with black on **`−`**. Keep the two wires and solder off the LED dome, holder-foot holes, board edge and other pad. [Adafruit's soldering examples](https://learn.adafruit.com/adafruit-guide-excellent-soldering/common-problems) show the difference between wetted, disturbed and bridged joints.
5. Turn the station off; let the star cool in the holder. Check that solder has wetted each pad and wire, with no loose strands or bridge. Hold the board and give each insulated lead a **gentle** tug: no movement at its solder pad. Place the 10734 holder loosely again; it must not pinch the wires or touch the LED dome.
6. If no meter is available, leave the two far wire ends **unstripped**, label the star **soldered, awaiting meter check**, and store it unpowered and unbonded. After the first star's visual, holder-clearance and gentle-tug checks pass, the second star can be soldered the same way and held at the same stage. When the meter is available, with each board disconnected from everything, strip about **2 mm** from each far end if it is still fully insulated; keep the two exposed tips apart. Check each free wire end to **its own pad**: near the same resistance as touching the probes together. Check there is **no hard zero-ohm short between `+` and `−` in both probe orientations**; an LED can make simple beeper/diode readings direction-dependent, so a beep alone is not a universal failure. If the result is unclear, leave the board unpowered and report the meter's displayed readings. After checking, cover the two exposed far ends separately for storage.

**Mechanical/visual pass:** both polarities match printed markings; both joints are wetted and immobile; the holder clears the wires; there is no visible bridge or stray strand. This permits soldering the second star, but leaves both electrically unverified. **Electrical pass:** each lead reaches its own pad and there is no hard two-way `+`/`−` short on the later meter check. **Fail/stop:** unclear pad polarity, damaged dome/board, lifted pad, loose wire, solder bridge, or ambiguous meter readings. Do not connect the 4S pack or the driver to diagnose it. Label the two checked boards `front` and `down` for later layout. Their optic holders, series link and powered driver test remain unreleased until the mounting/wiring package is checked.

## After a board's joint/meter pass — attach its star to its matching heatsink

[LEDSupply identifies HS13137](https://www.ledsupply.com/led-heatsinks/heatsink-finned-with-adhesive-tape) as a finned aluminum heatsink with a peel-off adhesive face for mounting one high-power LED. Its [XM-L2 star page](https://www.ledsupply.com/leds/cree-xlamp-xm-l2-leds) lists that heatsink as an accessory and describes the star board as electrically isolated. This supports the **star-to-heatsink** bond, not the separate attachment of a hot heatsink to PETG or the aircraft. The [10734 optic holder](https://www.ledsupply.com/led-optics/10734-carclo-lens-holder) needs its own adhesive and is not secured by the heatsink's tape.

1. Keep everything unpowered and fully cool. Confirm the part really is HS13137, its flat mounting face has an intact peel-off adhesive layer, and its finned side is clean and undamaged. If adhesive is absent, contaminated or peeling already, stop instead of substituting household glue or tape.
2. **Before peeling**, dry-center the checked 20 mm star on the heatsink's flat face. Temporarily rest the optic holder in place to verify both pigtails exit freely past it and will not be trapped **under** the star or against the heatsink edge. Choose the orientation now; do not plan on repeatedly lifting and re-sticking the star.
3. Remove the adhesive liner without touching the exposed tape. Lower only the star's flat **back** onto the adhesive, centered as rehearsed. Press gently and evenly on the metal star's outer areas, away from the LED dome, solder pads and wires. Follow any handling instruction supplied with the actual heatsink tape; do not heat the module to accelerate bonding.
4. Check the board lies flat and cannot slide with a light fingertip nudge. Recheck each free wire to its own pad for continuity, and look for any exposed wire strand or pad touching the heatsink. With the module still isolated, each `+`/`−` lead to the heatsink should show **no hard short** in both probe orientations; do not use a beep alone as proof of LED condition. If a reading is ambiguous, stop before any powered test. Store the module so nothing presses the dome or pulls its leads.

**Pass:** the star is centered and retained on the supplied tape, its two pigtails remain free and electrically continuous, and neither lead has a hard short to the heatsink. This is a completed **unpowered light subassembly**, not an installed or flight-qualified lamp. The optic holder still needs a chosen suitable adhesive, the sink needs positive aircraft retention and thermal separation from PETG, and the assembled lamp needs a later current-limited temperature/optical test before use.
