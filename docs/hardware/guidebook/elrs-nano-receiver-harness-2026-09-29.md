## Beginner USB-only receiver checklist (September 30)

The path for this test is **LiteRadio 4 (wireless) → Nano with attached T antenna → already-soldered black/red/green/yellow lead and its white 4-pin plug → original MicoAir743 `SBUS/CRSF` socket → FC USB cable → PC/Mission Planner**. Do not connect the flight battery, FC–ESC cable or propellers. The four colored wires require no separate plugging or resoldering. Radio USB stays unplugged from the PC. Owner already reported the white plug seated; all powered/radio results still await observation. On September 30 Windows showed COM7 (hub) and COM9 (radio USB), not the FC; its prior port was COM8, which must reappear or be newly identified when FC USB is attached.

1. With FC USB out, leave the already-seated Nano plug in the FC `SBUS/CRSF` socket and its T antenna on. The Nano board stays accessible on an insulated surface.
2. Put the **left throttle stick fully down**. On the LiteRadio 4, short-press and then hold the **front-center power button** until its lights come on. The official manual describes solid blue as working and solid red as a throttle-position warning. With the FC still unpowered, move throttle up once and return it fully down for firmware that activates RF on that movement. Use no radio USB cable.
3. Plug the **FC's** USB-C into the PC. Its lights and the Nano light may come on. Immediately remove FC USB if the receiver/FC heats, smells, or repeatedly disconnects. The Nano light color is less important than off/blinking/solid.
4. In open Mission Planner, select the **new FC COM port** (previously COM8; do not use the currently seen COM7 hub or COM9 radio), choose **115200**, click **CONNECT**, then `SETUP`/`INITIAL SETUP → Mandatory Hardware → Radio Calibration`. First observe, without clicking `Calibrate Radio`.
5. Move one control at a time: right stick left/right should move CH1, right stick up/down CH2, left stick up/down CH3, left stick left/right CH4, switch SA CH5, switch SB CH6. Live green bars are the control-data pass. A lit Nano alone is a power observation.
6. If no bars and Nano dark, remove FC USB and stop. If no bars and Nano blinking, disconnect Mission Planner, quickly power-cycle the **Nano by FC USB only** three times, leaving the third plug-in connected; expect a quick double blink. Long-press the LiteRadio 4's **underside BIND button, immediately left of its USB-C port**, within the bind window. The radio should give a success tone and the Nano should be solid. Reconnect Mission Planner and repeat the bars check. If Nano is solid but there are no bars, do not re-solder blindly; stop for exact signal/port/firmware diagnosis.
7. Only after all relevant bars move, run `Calibrate Radio` on the same screen with props/ESC/battery still absent: start, move sticks and SA/SB through full travel, select done, then center the spring-return axes, put throttle down and accept/save endpoints. For a separate receiver-loss check, turn radio off while still on FC USB and inspect Mission Planner `Messages` for an explicit RC-loss/failsafe indication; report the exact message rather than inferring failsafe from a frozen bar.
8. Disconnect Mission Planner, remove FC USB, then turn off the radio. This test does not replace deferred electrical short/continuity checks or complete flight readiness.

## The two ends and the electrical map

The [original MicoAir743 port drawing](https://micoair.cn/api/media/file/docs/2026/07/667c0fbaa3976-8a38aec13b-20c3f71554.webp) labels the four positions of the `SBUS/CRSF` socket on the FC's component face, with the printed arrow upward and that socket at the lower right, as `GND`, `5V`, `TX6`, `RX6` left to right **on the board drawing**. The [BETAFPV 2.4 GHz Nano receiver drawing](https://cdn.shopifycdn.net/s/files/1/1778/6615/files/c64abdf160319a144386c138470c9ecb_410768d9-19f9-461f-8b1b-2c399c61eede_600x600.jpg?v=1637059614) shows its pad face with the antenna jack at upper right and pads along the bottom: `RX`, `TX`, `VCC`, `GND` left to right **in that picture**. Read the markings on the received receiver before soldering; the picture does not establish an unknown variant's pad order. BETAFPV specifies a 5 V input and CRSF serial protocol for this receiver.

| FC socket function | Receiver pad | Connection |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `GND` | `GND` | Ground to ground |
| `5V` | `VCC` | Regulated 5 V supply |
| `TX6` | `RX` | FC transmit to receiver receive |
| `RX6` | `TX` | Receiver transmit to FC receive |

## Unpowered sequence

1. Use the owner-identified `ELRS 2.4G` BETAFPV Nano receiver and T antenna. **Set aside** its photographed four-color loose-wire bundle and separate black pin header. Find one actual four-conductor cable in the MicoAir stack accessory packet with a small **white keyed four-position plug** at each end; the package picture lists four such 15 cm cables, but the received contents must be checked. Before soldering, confirm the receiver's pad words against `RX`, `TX`, `VCC`, `GND` and that its antenna jack is intact. If the stack cable or an exact equivalent is absent, stop this receiver task and use the independent LED-star task; do not make a plug from loose wires and the header.
2. With FC, receiver, USB and battery disconnected and propellers off, put the FC approximately at frame center. On its connector/arrow face with arrow up, identify the lower-right socket printed `SBUS/CRSF`. Keep the four cut ends separate and gently seat the retained white plug in that socket for mapping only; do not force it. Place the loose receiver on an insulated support near the planned **rear inner arm** and confirm the already-cut cable still reaches with a relaxed bend, clear of motor/battery leads and sharp edges. If it is taut, move the receiver closer to the FC before soldering.
3. Using the seated plug and the no-meter method above, identify the color entering each FC board position left-to-right: `GND`, `5V`, `TX6`, `RX6`. Follow that color's uninterrupted insulation to the cut end and label its **function** on a small tape flag or paper chart. Ignore both earlier reported left/right lists. Then remove the plug from the FC. If a cavity-to-wire path is not visually clear, stop that wire; do not guess or solder it.
4. The receiver-end connector is already cut by owner report; **do not cut again**. Strip about 2 mm from each cut wire one at a time without nicking or losing strands. Keep bare tips separate. When a multimeter is available later, check its probes, then meter each FC-plug contact to exactly one intended receiver pad and verify no cross-continuity; never jam a thick probe into a 1 mm cavity. This meter result is mandatory before later receiver-connected plug-in/power, but the disconnected lead may be soldered after an unambiguous physical position map.
5. Cover the free ends other than the one in use. With the receiver supported by its **board edge**, lightly flux and tin one marked receiver pad and its mapped conductor. Attach `GND` to `GND`, then `5V` to `VCC`, `TX6` to `RX`, and `RX6` to `TX`, checking each cooled joint before moving to the next. Keep the iron away from the nearby radio components and antenna jack; if solder does not wet promptly, remove heat and let the board cool. No pin header is needed. If the map was visual-only, mark the finished lead **unverified—do not plug in/power** until Step 6's meter checks pass. Do not put heat-shrink over the receiver yet: [ExpressLRS asks for adjacent-pad inspection and an accessible initial powered check](https://www.expresslrs.org/quick-start/receivers/wiring-up/).
6. Let the board cool. Inspect all four joints for wetted pads, loose strands and solder bridges. Hold the board, gently tug **each insulated wire**, and meter each FC-plug contact to its intended receiver pad. Check neighboring pads and especially `5V`–`GND` for a persistent near-zero-ohm hard short with the board isolated. Electronics can give a transient/nonzero meter reading; report unclear numbers instead of calling a beep alone a pass. Leave the FC plug **unplugged** and the lead ends protected until the later receiver power/configuration card. Do not pull on the small antenna jack or use it for strain relief.

**Pass for disconnected preparation:** exact receiver/cable identity, relaxed route, an unambiguous physical conductor-position map and sound joints, if soldered. **Normal pass before plug-in/power:** each FC contact meters to its intended receiver pad, with no adjacent solder bridge or persistent supply short. The earlier USB-only diagnostic exception does **not** count as this full pass or release LiPo/flight work. **Stop:** different pad order/receiver band, marginal route, unreadable connector/wire position, damaged strands, bad joint, or ambiguous later meter result. Report which numbered check and the literal color order/reading/marking. A completed lead is a subassembly only; it does not establish receiver function or flight readiness.
