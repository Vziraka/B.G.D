## Frame coordinates and attachment

Use X right, Y forward between the red arms, Z upward, origin at frame center on the top of the lower PDB plate. Prototype lashing passages accommodate existing frame slots; they do not invent an exact F450 hole pattern. Do not drill the PDB. The attachment web must be real, continuous material with rounded/protected edges; a slot itself is not a supporting surface. No lashing may cross exposed solder, stack components or the sensor's optical apertures. Final dry fit establishes the actual lashing route.

Stack is centered at X=Y=0, adapter Z=0..6, X=±24, Y=±32. Feet are X=±21, Y=±25 and separate screw heads from the PDB. Four 8×4 tie passages are at X=±15,Y=±27, beyond the 36 mm FC outline; the ESC/harness envelope may be larger and must remain clear. Route four independent ties from each passage to a nearby existing frame slot/web and back around the adapter's end edge. Do not cinch a tie across a PCB or underneath an exposed bolt head. The four paths must resist both translation and rotation; foam friction alone is not retention. Thin electrical isolation under metal/near PDB pads is additional, not a substitute for clearances.

Flow support is installed under the lower plate, with its printed +Z facing downward: `translate([0,0,-plate_thickness]) rotate([180,0,0])`. CAD already places it on the right, X=29..81, Y=±19, with sensor case center X=65,Y=0. Root area X=29..53 is intended to contact the underside of the lower plate. Two separated root slots (X=36,Y=±11) allow lashing through existing plate slots and around the root's outboard edge; tie heads remain above or beside the plate, clear of components. No declared thickness value is needed for prototype generation. Do not trap a battery strap under its root. If the actual plate lacks a supported web/lashing path there, slide the support farther outward or revise only the root, preserving sensor X≥65 and secure root overlap. It is not permissible to support it on wire or empty space.

## Documented interfaces and calculated clearance

The [original MicoAir743 manufacturer page](https://micoair.com/flightcontroller_micoair743/) gives 30.5×30.5 mm mounting, 4 mm board holes, and 36×36×8 mm FC dimensions. Adapter holes are 3.4 mm for M3 screws through the **supplied silicone grommets**, not a replacement for them. The FC's 8 mm is not the assembled 50A stack height. Preserve isolation and keep connectors, solder joints and cables unloaded.

The owner reported roughly 38 mm clear interplate space. Reserving 5 mm clearance gives a 33 mm total installed ceiling. This adapter consumes **6 mm**, leaving **27 mm above its mounting face**, including all grommets, ESC, FC, nut and screw tips. Underside head clearance is 3.5 mm; a head must leave at least 0.5 mm separation from the PDB. A 30 mm screw seated against the plate underside at Z=3.5 rises to Z=33.5. The 0.5 mm excess is against our conservative allowance, **not proof that the purchased bolt physically hits the frame**; the tape gap is approximate.

**September 22 fastener selection:** specify four **M3×25 mm** screws (length below head, head height ≤3 mm) as the prototype design length, retaining the four ordered M3 nylock nuts and supplied dampers. The [exact ordered 50A stack package image](https://cdn.shopify.com/s/files/1/2778/6650/files/4_fd5a756c-be9d-4c25-b67a-d1a78e91e1cb.webp?v=1730401001) labels eight M3×8 mm silicone sleeves. With two 8 mm sleeves per screw, a 2.5 mm printed plate, a 4 mm nut allowance and 1 mm thread beyond the nut, the nominal requirement is **2.5 + 16 + 4 + 1 = 23.5 mm**. Choosing 25 mm leaves 1.5 mm axial reserve and puts the tip at Z28.5, approximately **9.5 mm below the upper plate**. The 4 mm nut allowance matches [Multicomp's M3 DIN985 drawing](https://www.farnell.com/datasheets/3213115.pdf); it is not a verified RDQ nut dimension. The sleeve-column calculation assumes installed boards sit within their grommet grooves without extra spacers. It supports a concrete starting selection, not certified complete-stack fit.

No bolt purchase or cutting is requested now. At installation, intact dampers must keep the boards/components separated and the nut's nylon insert must engage, with about two threads visible beyond it; do not crush rubber to obtain engagement. The 25 mm choice accommodates up to **17.5 mm between adapter top and nut bearing face** with the stated nut/thread allowances. Extra spacers or a different supplied damper geometry can change that limit; resolve any observed conflict then. Retain the 30 mm bolts as spares until fit is established. Move/revise the adapter or above-plate layout if the complete assembly cannot meet the 33 mm ceiling.

The [MTF-01P manufacturer page](https://micoair.com/optical_range_sensor_mtf-01p/) specifies 33.2×20.8×16.8 mm body, 24.3×12 mm hole pitch and 2.5 mm holes. Its flow field is 42°; rangefinder emission is 1.5°. Further inspection of the [current manufacturer manual](https://micoair.cn/zh/docs/sensors/sensors/mtf-01p-sensors) and its [dimension photograph](https://micoair.cn/api/media/file/docs/2026/07/66b4374b5dc9c-85c984aa0c-4b3c55e69c.webp) established that the four pictured corner locations already contain case screws. **The earlier vacant-mounting-ear assumption is withdrawn.** No manufacturer thread engagement or ear thickness was established. The revised CAD preserves those factory screws and holds the intact case externally; no sensor screw purchase or thickness measurement is needed.

The selected retention is **two of the already-ordered 150×3 mm nylon ties**, separate from the two frame-root ties. A 2.5 mm open-backed platform supports the case perimeter; 3 mm high corner stops provide a 34.2×21.8 mm clear rectangle, i.e. 1 mm total dimensional allowance. This is a design allowance, not a claimed case tolerance. The case's long side runs fore/aft. The center sides are open for the connector. Four 3×4 mm tie slots at X=65±13,Y=±13.5 guide two independent loops across the short dimension, around the **closed case end bands**. Insert the case rear against the platform with optics facing downward; latch tie heads on the support's rear/side away from the lens face and wires. Pre-thread loops before attaching to the frame where the inboard slot would otherwise be hidden. Tighten only until the intact case cannot move; do not distort the shell.

The manufacturer's overhead photograph shows the barrels within roughly the central ±10 mm fore/aft region. This photo-derived estimate leaves about 2 mm nominal separation to the inner edges of a 3 mm tie centered at ±13.5; slot and case movement can reduce it. **This optical clearance is inferred, not dimensioned by the manufacturer:** inspect the actual installed ties for no barrel/aperture contact and a clear view before use. The stops provide positive side/end restraint rather than relying on friction alone. If a tie can slide toward an aperture or the case cannot be restrained without shell distortion, revise only its guide location; do not force it, move optics or open the case.

Tie length is bounded without an exact case-depth measurement: using the entire nominal 16.8 mm optical-body height plus 1 mm allowance, 2.5 mm platform and a conservative 32 mm outer-width loop gives `2×(32+20.3)+20 = 124.6 mm`, including 20 mm locking/handling allowance. The purchased 150 mm tie leaves approximately **25.4 mm reserve**; actual end-band case height is lower than the full optical-body height. This resolves fastener selection with geometric slack. Verify the labelled sensor orientation in firmware during setup.

Battery interference calculation uses parent layout's conservative battery box X=±25.5,Y=±80 and lower extent Z=-57 mm. Allowing 0.5 mm inward motion within the cradle, the sensor body inboard edge is `65-20.8/2-0.5=54.1 mm`. Conservatively placing a lens anywhere on that edge at Z=0, the inboard flow cone at Z=-57 is `54.1-57*tan(21°)=32.22 mm`: **6.72 mm lateral margin** outside the battery side. The actual downward lens is lower and would improve that margin. This is a conservative envelope calculation, not a measured optical axis or complete arm/gear occlusion analysis. Battery-side accessories, bulging straps and end stops must also stay out of the cone. Lens-to-ground distance must exceed the manufacturer's 8 cm flow minimum; landing clearance alone does not establish it. Check the live sensor image/quality on textured ground after installation; no measurement request now.

## Components needing retention, not a printed box

- **Sequre GPS/compass:** reserve an upper rear location clear of the Pi and motor/battery wiring, with the antenna face open to the sky. Use the supplied protective case/sleeve if present and a rigid supported mounting face. Never strap directly across the ceramic antenna or a bare component. No exact bare-board tie-safe strip has been established; a stock cased module can be retained at its shell edges, while an uncased module needs a small insulated backing/keeper chosen from actual component locations. Do not invent a dimensioned cradle from an unverified photo. Powered compass interference is the consequential test; geometric separation alone cannot prove it.
- **BETAFPV ELRS Nano:** the [manufacturer](https://betafpv.com/products/elrs-nano-receiver) specifies a 12×19 mm board. Reserve about 25×20 mm plus connector/wire bend space near a rear inner arm. Retain the insulated module against a supported face without crushing parts, IPEX connector or solder joints; tie the insulated wire bundle separately as strain relief. Route the antenna into free space, held on its insulated feed cable and manufacturer's protected antenna structure. Do not compress the radiating T elements or regard the IPEX connection as a mechanical anchor. Exact tie placement follows visible component clearance, not an invented PCB keepout.
- **Matek BEC12S-Pro:** use its existing protective sleeve/case and supported isolated attachment if supplied; [manufacturer product information](https://www.mateksys.com/?portfolio=bec12s-pro) does not by itself establish safe strap pressure over every component. Place it away from the FC and optics, with open airflow and insulated solder tails. Cable strain relief bears cable loads. Do not wrap a bare inductor/PCB or bury the regulator in a printed enclosure or foam. A received bare module requires an insulated support matching the actual safe contact surfaces. Loaded temperature checks decide the separation from PETG, not a room-temperature drawing.

No final numerical positions for these three small parts are frozen before routing the actual harness. This is intentional layout flexibility; their retention is unfinished until component surfaces and cable lengths can be inspected during the already-needed installation.

## Small decisive physical checks

During the existing unpowered installation: establish continuous root/foot support and four/two secure lashing paths; confirm all documented holes fit without force; inspect screw-head/component isolation; confirm installed stack including screw tips leaves 5 mm clearance; verify the intact sensor case sits within its corner stops and both retaining ties miss the optical barrels/apertures and connector; verify its optical cone misses battery, straps and legs. Apply gentle handling loads in all directions without slip or cracking. Then props-off vibration and sensor/thermal observations precede flight. These are essential checks of unseen hardware and loads; no research or mesh test can substitute for them.

Print prototypes flat in PETG; the stack adapter's underside gap requires a print strategy (supports or inverted orientation) selected in the slicer, with support removed from bolt-head and tie clearances. The sensor support prints flat without needing a suspended roof. Slicer settings, printed mass and physical strength are not claimed verified.
