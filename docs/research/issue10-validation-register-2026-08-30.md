## Finding and closure boundary

**Measured hardware performance is an implementation/validation dependency, not a reason to leave planning open indefinitely.** The user's latest direction explicitly asks for physical requirements to be defined now and tested later. Close planning when the chosen conditional design, costs/risks, acceptance definitions, later-spec boundaries and failure gates are explicit and accepted. Do not label unperformed tests as passes, but do not demand a finished drone before writing its specification.

Q69 already approves the PC acceptance contract and separate flight gates. Q70 already permits the separate PC effort; its specification, test boundaries, eight implementation tickets and B-layout direction are now approved. Reopening those decisions would be redundant. Remaining PC model, operating-system integration and storage work belongs in those implementation slices. [Decision history](../../.scratch/biometric-guard-drone/issues/10-milestone-ladder.md#comments), [approved PC specification](../../.scratch/pc-biometric-desk/spec.md), [PC review/publication](../../.scratch/pc-biometric-desk/review.md)

Use exactly the user's two classes:

- **Answerable now:** requirements, conditional design, cost accounting, operating policy and test definitions. This also includes explicitly scheduled later-spec decisions and nonphysical procurement/consent/permission checks: their timing is later, but they do not require a flight experiment to decide. No selected costed conditional build, unpriced required features, an unaccepted overrun or a material capability tradeoff belongs in the physical-validation bucket.
- **Requires physical validation:** actual mass, power, endurance, pilot performance, target-device access, biometric attack resistance, mounting/thermal performance or reliability. These need evidence from the actual equipment, implemented system or consenting person. They may remain pending at planning closure when the selected design, test, evidence and disabled-on-failure capability are recorded.

The later milestones were intentionally approved at sketch level. Their detailed designs can remain assigned to named later-spec gates; issue 10 does not need to become five complete specifications. A gate is sufficiently explicit when it identifies the question to settle, the required artifact/test and the capability that cannot be enabled without it. It is not sufficiently explicit to say only “handle failures later.” The register below provides that assignment.

## Concrete proposed M1 aircraft acceptance definition

This is a **reviewable starting test envelope**, not universal safe distances or a ready-to-execute first-flight procedure. The exact aircraft, terrain, official requirements and physical assessment may require stricter limits or make this exercise unsuitable. The stated numbers are engineering proposals for repeatable evaluation; they are not ArduPilot-prescribed safety thresholds and not yet user-approved. If measurements cannot resolve a criterion reliably, the result is **not assessable**, not an estimated pass.

### Gate A — paper and simulator readiness

- The selected aircraft specification identifies every part/revision, power source, connection, motor/prop relationship, mounting arrangement, installed mass allowance, battery handling limits and supported firmware. Every safety-critical item has either a verified document basis or a named validation gate before use; no unidentified wire, unknown voltage rating or unresolved mechanical interference is carried into powered operation.
- The real radio is configured for the selected simulator and actual intended control mapping. Proposed simulator exit: **three consecutive complete virtual runs of the basic exercise below**, plus correct recognition and rehearsed response to lost orientation, a rejected mode change and an energy warning. This is separate from the already-approved three real-flight runs; no practice-hour quota is imposed.
- The pilot can explain their selected control modes, normal landing/disarm path, independent takeover and the distinction between emergency motor stopping and controlled landing. A kill switch is not ordinary low-battery protection. Unresolved control confusion blocks first flight; further simulator work remains possible.
- Freeze the commissioning plan and emergency/stop decisions before operating the aircraft. It must have independent preparation, very limited initial commissioning, post-flight review and only then repeatability evaluation. Do not combine the first unknown takeoff with the full acceptance exercise.

ArduPilot warns that initial untuned takeoff can be unusually dangerous and calls for simulator preparation and a stable tune before later testing. Its current guide uses Stabilize for initial commissioning, with altitude-control checks later; mode names alone do not establish the aircraft is ready. This report does not reproduce parameter changes or authorize their use. [ArduPilot initial tuning flight](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/initial-tuning-flight.html)

### Gate B — build and safe bench evidence

| Check | Passing evidence | Failure consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mechanical assembly | Documented secure fasteners/motor mounting, correct prop and frame clearance, no trapped/chafed wiring, retained battery/equipment, known centre-of-gravity envelope and no cracked/damaged parts; exact tightening criteria from selected components | No flight; repair/review and repeat affected checks |
| Electrical installation | Pinout/polarity independently checked against actual parts; no unintended short; regulators and loads within their published voltage/current limits; protected initial-power procedure defined; observed power rails and battery monitor within calibration tolerances fixed in the aircraft spec | No propulsion operation until corrected; no guessed electrical limits |
| Motor order and direction | Each actual output maps to the selected frame configuration and rotation; evidence from a reviewed **propellers-removed** bench method | No propeller-equipped operation; incorrect order/direction is not a tuning problem to discover airborne |
| Sensors/configuration | Actual orientation/calibration, frame class, receiver mapping, selected modes, logging and versioned parameters checked; all required flight pre-arm checks pass with none bypassed to obtain a flight | No takeoff |
| Radio/warnings/failsafes | Receiver truly signals link loss; exact firmware state transitions for radio loss, battery low/critical, telemetry loss, navigation faults and recovery are mapped and safely tested in simulation/bench conditions; no unintended continued mission, stale-command replay, cargo release or configured terminate action | No flight using unverified affected behavior |
| Energy/temperature | Battery chemistry/revision/health, connector and charge settings identified; limits and preliminary conservative landing protection fixed from documents/analysis before commissioning; temperature/current observations within selected component limits | Ground aircraft; no flight to deliberately find an empty-pack or overheating endpoint |

Motor-order testing requires removing propellers. ArduPilot's pre-arm documentation says failures must be resolved before flight; loose historical wording in other pages is not permission to bypass that gate. [Motor/ESC checks](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/connect-escs-and-motors.html), [pre-arm checks](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/common-prearm-safety-checks.html)

Radio and battery responses depend on firmware, configured modes and state; certain documented branches can disarm, so “a failsafe is enabled” is not sufficient evidence of the desired airborne behavior. The aircraft spec must audit the actual state table, use simulation/bench checks for faults, and keep dangerous deliberate airborne fault tests out of this novice acceptance plan. Battery protection requires working measurement and appropriately chosen thresholds; no generic example percentage/voltage is adopted. [Radio failsafe](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/radio-failsafe.html), [battery failsafe](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/failsafe-battery.html)

### Gate C — commissioning and proposed exercise envelope

Commissioning uses its separately reviewed, version-specific procedure; passing means controllable response and landing with no unexplained oscillation, warning, reset, damaged hardware, current/temperature exceedance or unresolved log finding. Passing a single lift is not commissioning completion. The subsequent acceptance exercise is proposed as follows:

| Parameter | Proposed baseline, pending review and configuration/site validation |
| --- | --- |
| Conditions | Dry daylight; measured local mean wind **≤2m/s**, observed gust **≤3m/s**; ambient **15–25°C**, further restricted by selected component limits. Recheck before each run and stop on deteriorating conditions. These wind numbers are project proposals, not a proven aircraft rating. |
| Practice area | Marked **10m × 10m** open flight box on level ground, with a separately assessed clear emergency margin. Proposed planning minimum: **20m unobstructed margin outside the box**, no uninvolved people/animals/vehicles in that margin, and pilot at least **15m from the nearest intended flight position** with an unobstructed exit. These distances do not establish adequate containment or legal separation; any larger actual requirement prevails. If safety assessment cannot justify the site, do not fly. |
| Visual/manual control | Pilot maintains direct visual contact, orientation and radio control; no phone launch, onboard verification, spoken-command session, following, orbit, delivery or night work in this M1 exercise. Simulator and actual mode mappings agree. |
| Mode | Proposed M1 acceptance uses **Stabilize** after successful commissioning in that mode. Any later substitution with an altitude/position-assisted mode requires its own successful commissioning and a frozen revised test protocol; stabilization remains permitted under Q42. |
| Hover | Nominal **3m above the local takeoff level**, remaining in a **2–4m** vertical band and within **2m horizontally** of the declared hover point for **30 seconds**. Measurement uncertainty must fit the decision; barometric/GNSS figures are not assumed exact. |
| Deliberate movement | At that same altitude band, move **5m** along a marked unobstructed direction, stop within **2m** of the endpoint for **5 seconds**, then return and stop within **2m** of the initial point for **5 seconds**. Keep the path in the box and away from the pilot; use no speed/racing challenge. |
| Landing | Controlled landing with final contact inside a marked **2m-radius** target that fits within the box, no collision/tip-over/damage, followed by confirmed landed/disarmed state. No person approaches moving motors. |
| Time/energy | Plan each basic exercise to last **no more than three minutes**; this is an exercise limit, not a battery endurance claim. Start only when the documented conservative energy allowance covers the planned exercise plus protected landing/uncertainty reserve. Earliest applicable energy/safety trigger ends the exercise even if the timer/count is unfinished. |
| Repeatability | Preserve Q62: **three consecutive complete qualifying real runs across at least two separately prepared sessions**. A skill miss resets the pass sequence. A prudent weather/site stop is not a skill failure but is not a pass; retain prior passes only if the aircraft/configuration is unchanged and the next session's full readiness checks pass. A safety fault pauses flight and requires affected revalidation; a configuration change restarts the three-run sequence. |

The temperature range reflects current ArduPilot initial-tuning advice; the narrower explicit exercise/weather/spatial/timing values otherwise are project proposals. The actual site's safety and measurement plan may disqualify them; changing a criterion must be documented **before** a new evaluation sequence, never to convert a recorded miss into a pass. [Initial tuning guidance](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/initial-tuning-flight.html)

Scale, electrical instrumentation, temperature/wind measurement and ground markings are required evidence resources, not assumed possessions. Their purchase/borrow/fabrication cost belongs in the full ledger. Forecast wind alone is not measured conditions at the aircraft. Logs plus after-flight observations are preferable to paperwork while flying; no helper, camera operator or in-person reviewer is assumed. AI cannot physically inspect the build or supervise a flight.

### Gate D — M1 manual-aircraft closure

Aircraft gates A–C must pass with unresolved safety faults cleared. The retired PC application is not a completion gate. Each real run has configuration/battery identifiers, conditions, preset criteria, per-maneuver results, energy/warnings, post-flight inspection and available logs; missing decisive evidence means incomplete. Passing M1 does not validate eleven-minute fully equipped missions, paired phone launch, onboard face/liveness, phone speech control, autonomy, night or delivery.
