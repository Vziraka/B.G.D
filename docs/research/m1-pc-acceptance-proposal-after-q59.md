## What is already required

The owner-only Windows desk app must perform the complete normal successful face-and-voice check within **10 seconds**, including prompts, with **at least 95% first-attempt genuine-owner success**, in ordinary indoor light and mild room noise. Natural speech, a visible face and clear eyeglasses if worn are included. Enrollment and retries are separate measurements. Any unauthorized approval in agreed security tests blocks sign-off until investigated, fixed and retested.

The host and verifier are assumed intact; photo/video, recorded/generated voice and software-substituted camera/microphone inputs remain in scope. Encrypted biometric references are retained; normal raw captures are discarded. Approval is for one requested action, with no later reuse. These are ticket requirements, not findings that the current devices or software satisfy them.

## Proposed finite first evaluation

1. **Prepare without using evaluation data.** Establish capture position and ordinary indoor conditions using a development session. Record the camera/microphone, hardware, Windows/app/model versions and ordinary power mode. Describe light/noise with reproducible room setup and available measurements; do not invent lux or decibel readings. Finalize thresholds, prompts, supported devices, retry handling and a numbered test matrix before evaluation. Freeze enrollment and configuration. No threshold changes, profile adaptation or cherry-picking during the evaluation.
2. **Run 100 fresh genuine-owner first attempts over five days:** 20 per day, split into two separated sessions of ten, with the agreed range of normal conditions distributed beforehand. Include the first check after reopening the app each session. Each attempt requests a new harmless desk action and starts fresh face-and-voice capture; repeated frames from one recording are not new trials. Enrollment is collected separately and never counted as evaluation. Day spread reduces dependence but does not make attempts statistically independent or representative of all future use.
3. **Measure two distinct outcomes.** Reliability passes the proposed observed criterion at **95 or more first-attempt authorizations out of 100**. Time from starting each requested check to displaying its result; every successful baseline check must be **at most 10 seconds** under this proposed timing interpretation. Report success count, slow-success count, failure durations and median/95th-percentile/maximum successful duration. A slow success remains a reliability success but fails timing; a fast rejection is not success. Record retries separately and never replace failed first attempts with their eventual successes. Any implementation timeout must be specified before testing, not used to erase failed trials.
4. **Run the security matrix below plus state-management checks.** Missing consent, attack tooling, Windows integration or required coverage means **incomplete**, not a pass. A single unauthorized approval blocks sign-off independently of the usability results.
5. **Preserve a result ledger, not normal biometric recordings.** Keep test identifiers, session/condition labels, durations, requested action, outcomes, configuration hashes and failure notes under a defined metadata policy. Log neither recovery secrets nor faceprints/voiceprints. After a fix, rerun the known failing case as regression evidence and evaluate the revised version with fresh held-out samples/sessions; do not call the old tuning material independent confirmation.

The sample counts and day schedule are practical engineering proposals, not prescribed standards. **95/100 observed success does not demonstrate that the true future success probability is at least 95%.** Report uncertainty and session clustering; use binomial intervals only with their independence/representativeness assumptions stated. Zero observed unauthorized approvals similarly cannot establish zero risk, particularly when attempts reuse people, recordings or tools. Do not combine heterogeneous attack cases into a universal security rate. NIST documents proportion intervals and small-sample exact methods. [NIST statistical handbook](https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/prc/section2/prc241.htm)

## Proposed security coverage

Before capture, inventory the exact sources, presentation devices and injection routes available. The following is a **minimum exploratory matrix, not evidence by itself that coverage is adequate**:

| Group | Initial proposed coverage | What must remain distinct |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Live nonowners | Three consenting adults, ten fresh attempts each across at least two sessions per person | Ordinary impersonation by real people. This tiny convenience sample cannot establish population false-match performance. Photos and public datasets do not replace it. |
| Face photographs | At least 12 full-gate attempts spanning at least three separately captured owner images, printed and screen presentation, and changed angle/light | Presentation to the physical camera; a public stranger image is not an owner-targeted spoof. |
| Face video | At least 12 attempts spanning at least three owner clips, changed playback settings and sessions | Include motion and any relevant prompt/challenge behavior; do not test only a trivially stale clip. |
| Recorded voice | At least 12 attempts spanning at least three owner utterances and varied playback level/distance | Acoustic playback into the microphone, distinct from digital audio substitution. |
| Generated/converted voice | At least 12 attempts across two materially different synthesis/conversion methods where obtainable with consent | Owner-targeted samples, including requested wording where relevant; a generic synthetic stranger voice alone is weak coverage. Unavailable methods remain an explicit gap. |
| Digital input substitution | At least 12 attempts split across camera-only, microphone-only and paired substitution, using more than one source sample | Exercise actual supported capture entry points without modifying the verifier decision code. A file passed directly to a model is only a component test. |

For each spoof family include **single-channel attacks while the consenting owner supplies the other genuine channel**, and paired attacks where applicable. Observe component and full-gate outcomes: a rejected face cannot establish that a simultaneous fake voice was detected. A working capture path and legitimate positive controls are necessary; a disconnected camera or an app that rejects everyone cannot pass security by default. Record all failures, source reuse and unavailable variations. The counts above imply 90 initial negative attempts, before any extra cases required by the actual design.

NIST's face PAD evaluation treats detection of photographs/video separately from identity recognition. ASVspoof separates physical replay, logical access and speech-deepfake evaluation and uses separate development/evaluation material. These distinctions support the matrix, not its proposed numerical counts. [NIST FATE PAD](https://pages.nist.gov/frvt/html/frvt_pad.html), [ASVspoof 2021](https://www.asvspoof.org/index2021.html)

NIST describes injection as insertion between capture and comparison and warns that biometric comparison and presentation defenses do not cover every such case. Therefore test real input substitution under Q19's intact-host assumption, explicitly recording attacker access; do not silently redefine it as verifier compromise or claim all injection routes are covered. [NIST digital injection guidance](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63a.html#digital-injection-prevention-and-forged-media-detection)

## Separate enrollment, storage and recovery checks

| Area | Concrete acceptance evidence to prepare |
| --- | --- |
| Setup and ordinary update | Fresh Windows verification binds the designated owner on deliberate first setup. Missing/corrupt profile data afterward does not reopen setup. Updating either reference requires both current biometrics and fresh owner Windows verification. Cancel/crash before commit preserves the last complete profile; success makes the replacement authoritative. Verify stored-profile version/state, not rejection of an old recording of the same owner, which could still match a newly enrolled reference. Ordinary verification never rewrites enrollment. |
| Recovery and code replacement | A valid code plus fresh designated-owner Windows verification allows lockout re-enrollment without requiring the failed drone biometrics. Neither alone suffices. Lost-code replacement requires both existing biometrics plus Windows verification, leaves references unchanged and invalidates the old code across restart. No valid code plus failure of either biometric stays blocked, including on an unlocked owner desktop. No route grants a normal action or arming authorization. |
| Windows failure semantics | Check offline success, wrong logged-on account, canceled/failed/unavailable verification, and account switching during the operation. Only the designated account's successful fresh verification satisfies its prerequisite. Microsoft describes verification of the logged-on user, so account binding must be proven separately; a launched prompt or successful API call is not the successful verification result. [Microsoft UserConsentVerifier](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/uwp/api/windows.security.credentials.ui.userconsentverifier?view=winrt-26100) |
| Confidentiality and integrity | Inspect the actual profile/key storage, ordinary-user access controls, logs, temporary files and crash artifacts after normal enrollment/checks and interrupted operations. References remain encrypted at rest; raw images/audio and secrets are not retained as normal history. Tampered/missing data denies authorization without opening ownership reset. Audit transient-copy handling; do not claim forensic erasure of every memory/pagefile/SSD copy from a file-deletion check. Host compromise remains outside the approved claim. |
| Freshness and interruption | Camera/microphone loss, cancellation and process restart discard partial evidence while preserving enrollment. A completed action approval cannot authorize a different request or be replayed after restart. Check both face-first and voice-first partial progress where supported. Recovery/update failures must not silently issue approval or leave ambiguous profile/code state. |

Recovery-code consumption/rotation after successful recovery, key handling, durable setup/invalidation state, retry limits/cooldowns and numeric freshness limits still need a concrete design. **No numerical security threshold or permanent-ban rule is approved by this report.** Engineering should propose these together with their usability consequences; Q23's combined-loss policy is not permission to permanently lock out a user after an arbitrary number of mistakes.

A temporary cooldown delays further attempts while preserving the enrolled identity; requiring re-enrollment instead changes the owner's recovery burden and may invoke Q23's lasting combined-lockout condition. NIST describes throttling and eventual disabling in its own assurance framework, but its particular numbers and fallback rules are not adopted for this project. First decide the intended failure policy, then choose and validate limits against guessing risk and owner usability. Repeated rejection is not proof that enrollment is corrupt. [NIST biometric attempt controls](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html#use-of-biometrics)

If adopted, time that complete workflow separately from the 100 verification attempts; count failed/restarted setup attempts and report the owner's ability to finish without developer intervention. This is neither a measured result nor a permission to collect samples.

## Decisions needed from the user, versus engineering work

- **Evaluation effort:** Is the proposed five-day owner check schedule, plus arranging consenting live nonowner checks, practical? The user need not supply model thresholds or lighting engineering numbers. If nonowners are unavailable, retain that coverage gap and do not declare full M1 security acceptance.
- **Test-data permission, separately before capture:** Obtain explicit permission for the specific temporary owner recordings, any owner-targeted synthetic media and each volunteer's participation; present where data stays, who can access it, a deletion deadline and whether any external service is involved. Recommend local-only test assets, no cloud uploads, deletion after the agreed evaluation/retest window, and retention of non-biometric result metadata only. This recommendation does not itself authorize collection or settle the exact retention window.

Engineering owns translating these into a reproducible matrix, selecting and integrating capture/verification software, constructing controlled injection tests and demonstrating storage/recovery behavior. Existing HP camera/IR device names and GPU inventory do not demonstrate liveness, accessible infrared frames, trusted capture, Windows verification integration or adequate speed.

Finally, NIST SP 800-63B-4 requires a physical authenticator alongside biometric comparison in its context and prohibits voice-based biometric comparison. The chosen face-and-voice gate **must not be represented as NIST-compliant**; those references inform evaluation distinctions without replacing the user's policy. Passing this bounded proposal would support a documented first-PC evaluation only, not immunity to unseen attacks or permission to arm aircraft motors. [NIST use of biometrics](https://pages.nist.gov/800-63-4/sp800-63b.html#use-of-biometrics)
