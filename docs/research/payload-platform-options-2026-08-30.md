# Payload-platform options — bounded primary-source comparison

Checked **2026-08-30** in the main checkout, branch `main`, base commit `68e00796f56d1975b05efa8e8f3c5c5fd504b6ac`. This is **new, uncommitted research**, not content of that commit or an approved design. No purchases, installations, configurations, construction, tests or flights occurred.

## Decision-relevant finding

**Larger aircraft are credible comparison directions, but the checked manufacturer evidence does not establish a safe carrying limit, the required endurance, or a complete-project fit below $800.** A cheaper 9-inch platform publishes only twenty minutes of maximum hover; a 10-inch platform advertises longer hover but costs more than the entire budget before batteries or project equipment. Neither is a substitute shopping list. No complete-platform test with fully specified cargo, battery, installed equipment, conditions and protected reserve was found in this bounded check.

The **self-built 7-inch aircraft and two new LiPos remain the baseline**. Larger diameters, ready-built aircraft and Li-ion are unapproved comparisons. The reference remains **20 useful minutes plus a separate return/landing reserve, without extra delivery cargo**, with camera/computer installed, initially within 100m in dry/daylight/calm conditions. The **$600 complete-build goal and conditional $800 absolute maximum** cover all milestones and initial equipment; later wear/repairs are separate. Q63–Q64 remain unanswered. No cargo cap is adopted.

## New evidence catches: revision and propeller matching

The manufacturer's separate Helion marketing page describes **950±20g without battery/GoPro**, **17min with a 5200mAh6S LiPo**, and thirty minutes using an 8000+mAh Li-ion. Those differ from the current O4 listing's mass, pack and hover figures. They may describe different versions/workloads; do not combine the lighter mass from one with the longer time from another. Neither page specifies a complete reproducible flight protocol. [Manufacturer Helion overview](https://www.iflight.com/en-helion-10.html)

The [Helion3110 motor page](https://shop.iflight.com/Helion10-3110-Motor-Pro2136) lists **$45.99 each**, in stock, or **$183.96 for four** before delivery. Its linked manufacturer test image was visually inspected. The **10-inch test is HQ10×5×3, not the aircraft's named Gemfan1050**; its lowest listed point is **789g at24.91V, 9.95A, 247.9W**. This does not provide matched low-hover data for the advertised platform or authorize extrapolation to another propeller. The published motor mass is77g including wire. [Manufacturer test image](https://shop.iflight.com/image/catalog/TEST%20REPORT/XING2-3110-900KV.png)

Thus the exact-propeller and operating-point checks still matter even when a manufacturer sells the motor and aircraft together. Peak thrust is **not** a payload rating: it does not validate sustained electrical/thermal operation, control margin, structure, landing behavior or secure retention.

## What 250g, 500g and tentative 3lb mean

For a checkable scale comparison only, retain the previous **unmeasured 1298.4g** LiPo-equipped 7-inch estimate. It includes a provisional camera/computer allowance and remainder, not a verified completed mass. The calculations below exclude any **additional** secure holder, release mechanism and packaging:

| Cargo example — not an approved limit | Illustrative combined mass | Increase over that baseline |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 250g | 1548.4g | 19.3% |
| 500g | 1798.4g | 38.5% |
| Tentative3lb =1360.777g | 2659.177g | 104.8% |

These are materially different engineering workloads, not predicted flight times. **250g is a useful lower-load comparison, not yet a safe recommendation; 500g needs its own validation; 3lb approximately doubles this illustrative aircraft's mass before the holder.** A larger airframe does not make any row automatically acceptable. Its heavier propulsion, frame and battery consume part of the capacity gained. Cargo position, dimensions and drag also remain unknown; naming a particular item is not required to continue comparison work.

Q55–Q58 require the intended recipient's face before release, protection against ordinary hand-removal, a landed/motors-stopped handoff, then remaining disarmed for someone to carry the drone back. **A strap or ordinary gripper alone is not demonstrated compliance.** A secure holder, landing clearance, release hardware and their installation must enter the mass/cost/power assessment. Safe landing and independent emergency control remain independent of recipient verification; any new arming still requires owner authorization. No force/theft-resistance claim is made.

## Recommended next work, not a selected redesign

Keep the baseline active while comparing a fully costed 7-inch integration against an explicitly unapproved self-built larger-diameter option. Use identical required equipment and separately account for delivery hardware, cargo and no-cargo endurance. Obtain exact propeller/voltage data, complete installed mass, relevant battery limits and recovery-energy evidence before choosing a battery or carrying limit. The current findings justify **further comparison**, not asking the user to enlarge the frame on an unsupported promise, adopting a smaller cargo cap, or purchasing parts. Any eventual change of size or chemistry requires explicit approval. No changes to the approved milestone sequence or unanswered M1 questions follow.
