# Landing policy and battery reserve — decision support

Date checked: **2026-08-30**. Scope: ticket 10; current ArduPilot Copter documentation. Status: research and proposed design, **not an approved flight configuration**. Authored directly in the main checkout; no source worktree or implementation changes.

## Current decision context

The requested endurance is approximately twenty minutes of useful flight with the future camera/computer onboard, following at walking pace with occasional stops, **plus a separate return-and-landing reserve**. The latest user answer accepts a provisional **100 m radius from takeoff for the first endurance test**, while retaining greater eventual range. The user also proposes selectable home/commanded-spot/near-owner landing and asks about spending the reserve before a very-low-battery automatic landing. These proposals require a decision; this report does not approve removing the reserve or reducing the roadmap. The complete $600 lifetime fit remains unverified.

## Documented behavior

- **Low battery and critical battery are separate layers.** Copter supports a first battery-triggered action and another at a lower voltage or remaining-capacity threshold; the documented example is return first, land at critical. Battery sensing must work. These triggers do not establish how much energy a particular route will consume. A mode change can let the pilot retake control, and the same already-triggered battery failsafe does not simply re-trigger; the second layer matters. This is a control-boundary warning, not a recommendation to cancel protection. [ArduPilot battery failsafe](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/failsafe-battery.html)
- **Ordinary Land is a descent behavior, not a ground-safety assessment.** It attempts to descend where the aircraft is, with horizontal position control when a suitable position estimate is available and different behavior without GPS lock. Pilot repositioning depends on configuration. Landing detection concerns vehicle motion, thrust and available contact/range observations; it does not establish that the ground is free of people, water, wires or unsuitable surfaces. That last distinction is an inference from the documented controller, not a claim that site-recognition software exists. [ArduPilot Land mode](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/land-mode.html)
- **Flying to a spot and descending are distinct operations.** Guided navigation can take the vehicle to a requested location and then hover. Ordinary RTL instead returns toward home or a configured rally point, requires a reliable position estimate and can include climbing before the return leg. Its default direct route is not automatically a safe obstacle-avoiding route. Neither behavior proves that the destination is suitable for touchdown. [Guided mode](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/ac2_guidedmode.html), [RTL mode](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/rtl-mode.html)
- **A moving owner is not automatically the return point or a landing target.** Follow mode consumes another entity's reported position over telemetry and supports an offset and a maximum following distance. This is not equivalent to identifying a person in camera pixels, obtaining that person's reliable position, or checking nearby landing ground. Position freshness, coordinate frames, estimation error and loss handling must be addressed by the eventual implementation. [Follow mode](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/follow-mode.html)
- **Precision landing adds targeting, not automatic site approval.** Copter can use an external landing-target estimate for final descent. It needs sound position/attitude estimates; target loss can lead to retries, ordinary landing or hovering, depending on configuration. Moving targets require separate handling. A marker or accurate coordinate is therefore not proof of clear ground, and retries/hovering can consume remaining energy. [Precision landing](https://ardupilot.org/copter/docs/precision-landing-and-loiter.html)

## Recommended design for the next decision — not yet ratified

1. Make **destination selection** configurable; keep **safe-recovery energy** protected. A closer validated landing site can reduce the travel component of the reserve, but cannot eliminate energy for approach, controlled descent, touchdown and uncertainty.
2. Accept a changed destination only while it is reachable with conservative remaining energy and a clear, suitable landing area. Recompute the route and reserve when the destination changes. Energy already spent against the original return plan cannot be recovered by changing a menu selection; if no suitable alternative is reachable, “land near me” is not a reliable fallback.
3. Interpret “near owner” as a **separate clear landing site at a safe separation**, not descent onto or immediately beside the person. For the initial implementation, recommend owner confirmation of a stationary site before the recovery phase. An owner who has moved requires an updated destination assessment; touchdown relative to a continuously moving target would be a separate unvalidated capability, not something implied by Follow mode or selected here.
4. Warn early enough to act, begin recovery before the recovery allowance is consumed, and retain a critical fallback. Do not make safe landing depend on the user noticing an alarm. Do not promise a late autonomous search for clear ground when almost no energy remains.
5. Preserve **emergency manual override** for immediate avoidance and landing without another biometric check. That does not grant routine mission commands permission to spend the protected reserve, restore battery energy, or bypass arming authorization for a new flight. Validate the interaction between manual control, recovery and both battery layers in simulation before flight testing.

These are engineering recommendations derived from the documented mode distinctions, not native ArduPilot guarantees. No fixed voltage/percentage thresholds, disable-failsafe instructions or parameter changes are selected here.

## Unresolved validation and cost

Measure loaded power and usable battery capacity under the representative workload, battery condition, wind and temperature; account for return-route altitude changes, approach and uncertainty. Define landing-site clearance, positioning accuracy/freshness, accepted destination changes and behavior after target/link/position loss. Select and price any additional positioning, downward sensing or site-assessment hardware only after those requirements are settled. Neither twenty useful minutes plus reserve nor autonomous safe-site selection has been demonstrated within $600; greater range remains an aspiration requiring a new validated operating envelope and applicable permissions.
